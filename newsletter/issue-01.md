Subject: The $3 jacket somebody else put back

Luck Ledger #1

At 7:30 on an ordinary Thursday morning, Quinn Brown was at the Goodwill bins in Portland. He was 19 and resold old clothes for a living.

An older reseller next to him picked up an old Lakers warm-up jacket, looked at it and tossed it back. Brown grabbed it "in half a second." He knew one thing for sure: 70s sports gear is always worth something.

He paid about $3.

It turned out to be the jacket Wilt Chamberlain wore in the 1972 NBA Finals. Brown posted it on Instagram, and that's how Sotheby's found him. On 20 July 2026 it sold at Sotheby's for $89,600.

Two people held the same jacket that morning. One of them put it down.

## The move

**Move 26: Know the field well enough to see the odd result.**

The jacket was in plain sight. Anyone could have bought it for $3. What Brown had, and the other reseller didn't, was enough knowledge of his corner of the world to see that this particular piece of junk was worth a second look.

There's more to it, though. Brown goes to the bins three or four times a week and lists about 30 items a day, so he gets a lot of tries. And because he put the jacket on Instagram, the right people could find him. Knowing the field told him what to grab, and posting it brought the buyer to him.

## Your 10 minutes this week

Pick the one area you know better than most people around you. It could be your industry, a hobby or a tool you use every day.

Write down one thing in it that looked odd to you lately. Maybe it was a strange result, a complaint you keep hearing, or something cheaper or more popular than it should be. Then spend ten minutes asking why.

Most of these go nowhere. Brown passed on plenty of jackets before this one.
